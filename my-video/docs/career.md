# Editing for clients

Notes for when you edit for other people, not just your own page.

## Two routes
| Route | The work | Catch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clipper | Take long videos (streams, vlogs, podcasts), cut many short clips with captions, post everywhere | easy to start, so thousands compete on price |
| Content partner | Research, write the scripts, help film, edit, post: the whole production | harder, but busy business owners pay for exactly this |

## Stand out
- **Have a second skill.** Editing alone competes with $10 editors. Writing is the strongest pair: write the script, the client only records. Then sell results (views, followers, leads), not "nice edits".
- **Make professionals into creators.** Don't hunt for famous creators to edit for. Doctors, lawyers, coaches and shop owners have the knowledge; content brings them clients. Turn whoever you can into a creator.
- **Niche = what you never get tired of.** Not "the best-paying niche". If you'd watch game trailers all day, edit game trailers; you'll know what works better than anyone.
- **Startups over big companies.** In a small team you also do strategy, writing, filming and posting, so you can say "my videos brought this many views", which makes a raise or a move easy.

## Get found
- **Instagram is the job board.** Your page is your CV, portfolio and brand at once. Post consistently and clients come to you.
- **Show the timeline.** When you post your own work, show the editing timeline (the `edit-timeline` style, or a screen recording of Remotion Studio) with a short note on what you did. It tells people "this is an editor's work" instantly.
- **Will AI replace editors?** It already cuts footage and adds captions well (that's what this repo does), and it's strong at motion design. It's still weak at writing. Spend your learning time on the fundamentals it can't do for you: the script, the hook, taste.

## A client edit, start to finish
1. A-roll: the client records the script.
2. Basic cuts: pauses and fillers out automatically (`npm run cut`).
3. B-roll every 3–5 s; if the client sends none, build a "b-roll space" (`explainer` with `color`, icons, photos) in their brand colours.
4. Captions chosen for *their* audience's age (`docs/editor-rules.md`).
5. Sound, music, effects last and lightly: they support the message, never compete with it.
